## Reproduced failures and changes

The original search stopped at 24 early matches, missing a rare implementation
later in the inventory. It also let the first query exhaust a file's four-hit
allowance before later queries were examined. Both cases failed before the fix.

Search now scans within the existing 8 MiB budget before choosing output. It
prioritizes underrepresented files and queries, keeps at most 24 output hits and
four hits per file, and bounds its internal pool to 200 × 6 × 4 snippets. Repeated
matches on one line are skipped after the first excerpt, avoiding redundant
excerpt construction. No provider calls, network access, or paid routes are added.

Trade-off: search can read more of the bounded inventory instead of stopping
after early matches. Evidence outside the 200-file/8-MiB limits can still be missed;
this is not an exhaustive symbol index or semantic relevance ranking.

## Quality claims and remaining evaluation

No live provider response or competing AI was evaluated here. Use identical
held-out tasks, repositories and time budgets to compare task success, regression
rate, citation support, latency and actual cost. Include quota failures and
unsolved tasks. Production Postgres concurrency, complete deployed job flows,
automatic recovery dispatch and Git delivery still need their own evidence.
Passing these tests does not authorize a claim of Claude Code parity or superiority.
